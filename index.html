Map settings
Changing the map size resamples the image (Undo brings it back). Brightness is height 0..1 scaled by Max height, so changing Max height stretches the whole map. Vertex size only feeds the memory numbers below (your engine's vertex format).
Generate a base
Seeded noise to start from, then paint over it. Same seed and settings = same map. This replaces the brightness (height) of every pixel and keeps the hues unless Auto biome is on (Undo brings it back).
Auto biome
Re-colors every pixel from its height and slope, keeping its brightness: water below sea level, sand on the shore, rock on steep slopes, snow up high, forest and grass in between. Undo brings it back; paint over it after.
File
Your map autosaves in this browser. The map PNG is the map: hue = biome, brightness = height. It also carries the map settings (m/px, max height, sea, chunk) inside the PNG, so opening it here restores everything. You can paint it in any pixel editor too (keep it lossless: PNG, no JPEG, no smoothing).

Terrain Maker v2

The world map is one color image. Each pixel is one terrain vertex:

  • Hue = biome. Every biome has a key color (the swatches). A pixel belongs to the biome whose key color has the closest hue, no matter how bright or dark the pixel is.
  • Brightness = height. The biome's full key color is Max height. Darker shades of that color are lower, down to 30% brightness at 0 m, never black.
  • Grey and white are Rock; Snow is the icy blue-white. Pure black (no hue) counts as Water at 0 m.

The 30% floor is there so dark, low pixels still show their hue: deep water is dark blue and low forest is dark green, not black. It costs a little precision: there are still about 290 to 530 height steps per biome (0.4 m steps at 120 m Max height).

Water

Sea level (Map settings) decides where the sea is: the 3D water plane sits there, and Auto biome colors everything below it Water. The Water biome is the color of wet ground (sea floor, lake or river bed), and its brightness is the bed height like any other biome, so deeper water is darker blue. A Water pixel above sea level is a lake or river bed.

Tools

  • Raise / Lower brighten or darken the pixels and keep their hue. Smooth blurs heights. Flatten pulls toward the height where the stroke started, which is good for plateaus and build sites.
  • Biome repaints the hue with the selected swatch and keeps each pixel's brightness (height). Fill does that for a connected area of one biome. Pick takes the biome under your finger.
  • Generate: seeded noise as a rough start. Auto biome: hues from height and slope.
  • Map: size, meters per pixel, max height, sea level and chunk size. The top-left line shows the world size and what the meshes will cost.
  • 3D: the live low-poly preview, flat shaded, drawn with each biome's game color.
  • File: save / open the map PNG, the palette, a grey heightmap. Export: a C header with compact height + biome grids and the palette for your game.

Controls

  • Map: one finger / left mouse paints. Two fingers pan and pinch-zoom. Mouse: right or middle drag pans, wheel zooms.
  • 3D: one finger / left drag orbits, two fingers pan and zoom. Mouse: right drag pans, wheel zooms.
  • Keys: Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y undo / redo, [ and ] brush size, 1-7 pick a biome.

Coordinates

Z is up. The image is a top-down map with north (+Y) at the top: pixel (col, row) sits at world x = col × m/px, y = (H − 1 − row) × m/px, z = height.
