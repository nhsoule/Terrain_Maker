Map settings
Map size
Each pixel is one vertex, so the world is (pixels − 1) × m/px wide: 1025 px at 2 m is 1024 cells = 2048 m = exactly 32 chunks of 32 cells (1001 px at 2 m is exactly 2000 m). Width and height can differ (16 to 4096 px). Scaling resamples heights smoothly and biomes by nearest pixel; crop / pad keeps every pixel 1:1 and fills new area with sea floor (Min height). Undo brings the old size back.
Terrain
Heights are stored in meters between Min and Max height (changing the range clamps, it never stretches the map). The export spreads its height steps over this range. Auto ocean: ground below sea level that connects to the map edge (or to painted Ocean) is ocean, so raising or lowering the coast moves the shoreline by itself; dips inland stay dry unless you paint them as Lake or Pond. Vertex size only feeds the memory numbers below (your engine's vertex format).
First person (Player)
Match these to your game's camera to judge the map at the size a player sees it. Press Player, then click / tap where to stand. Water: you wade until it is deeper than eye height − 0.3 m, then swim with your eyes 0.3 m above the surface (half speed).
Edge fog
Light fog that thickens toward the map edge over this distance, so the edge fades into haze instead of ending abruptly. Shown in the orbit view and first person (also the Edge fog button in the 3D view). Preview only, not exported.
Generate terrain
A designed-looking base to sculpt on: big landforms from warped noise, ridged mountain ranges, then erosion that cuts gullies and settles slopes. Same seed + settings = same map, on every device. Undo brings the old map back.
Auto biome rules
Paints the biome layer from the terrain, top rule first. Heights are never touched. Hand-painted water (Lake, River, Pond, Swamp, Ocean) is kept unless you untick it.
Below sea level becomes sandy sea floor (Desert; Auto ocean shows it as Ocean). Everything else that no rule takes is Plains.
File
Your map autosaves in this browser. A project (.tmproj) is the whole map in one file: both layers at full precision plus every setting.
Layers as images
Height PNG: 16-bit greyscale, black = Min height, white = Max height (Gaea, World Machine, Krita, Photoshop read and write it). Importing scales it to the map size and keeps the biomes. Biome PNG: each pixel in its biome's key color (the swatch colors); paint it anywhere with a hard brush, then import it back (nearest color wins). Importing keeps the heights.
Game exports
Open an exported .h or .bin (any of the 3 formats, from this or an older version) to keep editing it: size, m/px, height range, sea level, chunk size and biomes come back. Water is recomputed from the biomes. A .bin does not store the Min height, so you are asked for it. You can also drop a file anywhere on the page.
Open also reads maps from older Terrain Maker versions (the one-image map PNG where hue = biome and brightness = height, and .json projects). They are split into the two layers.
Import .bin
A .bin stores heights above the bottom of its range, not the Min height itself. Leave 0 unless you exported with a Min height below 0 (the .h has it as height_offset).

Terrain Maker

Build a low-poly world for your game: generate a base, sculpt it into a designed place (plateaus, ramps, rivers, build sites), let auto biome paint it, touch up by hand, check it in first person, then export. Every pixel is one terrain vertex, and the 3D view is the exact full-resolution mesh.

Two layers

  • Height: meters, between Min and Max height (Map settings). Sculpt tools only change this layer.
  • Biome: one of 10 biomes per pixel: 0 Ocean, 1 Lake, 2 River, 3 Pond, 4 Swamp, 5 Desert, 6 Snow, 7 Plains, 8 Rock, 9 Forest (keys 1-9, 0). Biome tools only change this layer.
  • The buttons at the bottom right of the map switch the view: Height (shaded relief) or Biome (biome colors over the relief, opacity slider), and Contours (a line every few meters, every 5th darker).

Workflow

  1. Generate: pick a preset (Island, Continent, Mountains & valleys, Rolling hills) and a seed. Erosion runs in the background for a few seconds.
  2. Sculpt the big moves first with a large brush: Raise / Lower whole areas, Flatten plateaus and build sites, Ramp roads between them, River to carve valleys to the sea. Then Smooth, Roughen or Terrace for the finish.
  3. Auto biome: rock on steep slopes, snow up high, beaches by the water, forest patches. Re-run it after sculpting; your hand-painted water stays.
  4. Biome brush / Fill for the details: lakes, ponds, swamps, a desert.
  5. Player: walk it at your game's eye height and FOV.

Sculpt tools (height layer)

  • Raise (R) / Lower (L): push the ground up or down under the brush.
  • Smooth (S): soften bumps and sharp edges.
  • Flatten (V): level to the height where the stroke starts, or to a typed height (tick fixed). Alt+click (long press on touch) samples the height under the cursor.
  • Ramp (P): drag a line; it builds a smooth, evenly graded road / ramp from the start height to the end height. Size = road width, Falloff = how far the embankment blends out.
  • Roughen (N): adds natural bumps (detail size in meters).
  • Terrace (T): turns slopes into flat shelves with steep steps (step height in meters), a strong low-poly look.
  • River (C): drag a path. It carves a channel that always runs downhill (from the higher end to the lower end), with sloped banks, and paints it River. Size = channel width.
  • Brush Size is in meters (each tool remembers its own), Strength, and Falloff (0 = hard edge, 1 = very soft). Pen pressure scales the strength.

Biome tools

  • Biome (B) brush, Fill (G) a connected area of one biome, Pick (I) a biome (Alt+click works too).
  • Select (M): paint a soft selection (Alt erases). Generate can blend into it, Auto biome can be limited to it.

Water

  • Ocean is automatic: ground below Sea level that connects to the map edge is sea (Map settings: Auto ocean). Sculpt the coast and the shoreline follows.
  • Lake / Pond: paint the biome in a dip; each patch fills to its spill level (the lowest ground around it, at least 0.3 m deep).
  • River: 0.8 m over the bed, always flowing downhill to where it meets Ocean, Lake or Pond. The River tool carves the bed for you.
  • Swamp: wet ground with a 0.25 m film of water.

First person (Player)

  • Press Player, then click / tap a spot on the map or in the 3D view. Desktop: click the 3D view to look with the mouse, WASD / arrows move, Shift sprints, Space jumps, Esc exits. Touch: left thumb joystick, drag on the right to look, Jump button.
  • You walk on the exact mesh; you wade, then swim in deep water. Eye height, FOV, speeds and clip planes are in Map settings. Edge fog fades the map edge into haze.

Controls

  • Map: left mouse / one finger uses the tool. Right or middle drag pans, wheel zooms; two fingers pan and pinch-zoom. F fits the map, = is 1 screen pixel per map pixel.
  • 3D: drag orbits, right drag pans, wheel zooms; two fingers pan and zoom. Sculpt in 3D (E) uses the current tool on the terrain instead: then right drag orbits and middle / Shift drag pans.
  • Keys: tools R L S V P N T C B G I M; [ ] brush size, , . strength, 1-9, 0 biome, H height / biome view, K contours, E sculpt in 3D, Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y undo / redo.

Files and export

  • File: project (.tmproj), 16-bit height PNG and biome PNG in and out, and older one-image maps (converted to two layers).
  • Import .h / .bin (or drop it on the page): reopens a game export of any format or version for editing. Water is recomputed; a .bin asks for the Min height.
  • Export: a C header or a .bin with the full grid, biome ids, water surface and chunk info. Formats: uint16 height + uint8 biome, uint8 + uint8, or packed uint16 (h = v >> 4, b = v & 15).
  • Coordinates: Z up, north (+Y) at the top of the map. Pixel (col, row) is world x = col × m/px, y = (H − 1 − row) × m/px.
