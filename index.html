Map settings
Map size
Each pixel is one vertex, so the world is (pixels − 1) × m/px wide: 1025 px at 2 m is 1024 cells = 2048 m = exactly 32 chunks of 32 cells (1001 px at 2 m is exactly 2000 m). Width and height can differ (16 to 4096 px). Scaling resamples heights smoothly and biomes by nearest pixel; crop / pad keeps every pixel 1:1 and fills new area with sea floor (Ocean, 0 m). Undo brings the old size back.
Terrain
Brightness is height 0..1 scaled by Max height, so changing Max height stretches the whole map. Vertex size only feeds the memory numbers below (your engine's vertex format).
First person (Player)
Match these to your game's camera to judge the map at the size a player sees it. Press Player, then click / tap where to stand. Water: you wade until it is deeper than eye height − 0.3 m, then swim with your eyes 0.3 m above the surface (half speed).
Edge fog
Light fog that thickens toward the map edge over this distance, so the edge fades into haze instead of ending abruptly. Shown in the orbit view and first person (also the Edge fog button in the 3D view). Preview only, not exported.
Generate a base
Seeded noise to start from, then paint over it. Same seed and settings = same map. This replaces the brightness (height) of every pixel and keeps the hues unless Auto biome is on (Undo brings it back).
Auto biome
Re-colors every pixel from its height and slope, keeping its brightness: Ocean below sea level, Desert sand on the shore, Swamp on some low flat land near the sea, Rock on steep slopes, Snow up high, and Forest and Plains in between. Lakes, rivers and ponds are yours to paint (this replaces any already painted). Undo brings it back; paint over it after.
File
Your map autosaves in this browser. The map PNG is the map: hue = biome, brightness = height. It also carries the map settings (m/px, max height, sea, chunk) inside the PNG, so opening it here restores everything. You can paint it in any pixel editor too (keep it lossless: PNG, no JPEG, no smoothing).
Open: a PNG of 16..4096 px on each side becomes the map at its own size (any shape); bigger images are resized to the current size. Colors that aren't exact palette colors snap to the nearest biome hue (their brightness is kept). Older Terrain Maker v1 .json projects open too. Grey heightmap: any image, brighter = higher; it replaces the heights and keeps the biomes.

Terrain Maker

The world map is one color image. Each pixel is one terrain vertex:

  • Hue = biome. Every biome has a key color (the swatches). A pixel belongs to the biome whose key color has the closest hue, no matter how bright or dark the pixel is.
  • Brightness = height. The biome's full key color is Max height. Darker shades of that color are lower, down to 30% brightness at 0 m, never black.
  • Grey and white are Rock; Snow is the icy blue-white. Pure black (no hue) counts as Ocean at 0 m.

10 biomes (id: name): 0 Ocean, 1 Lake, 2 River, 3 Pond, 4 Swamp, 5 Desert, 6 Snow, 7 Plains (grassy), 8 Rock, 9 Forest. Keys 1-9 and 0 pick them in this order.

The 30% floor is there so dark, low pixels still show their hue: deep water is dark blue and low forest is dark green, not black. It costs a little precision: there are still about 250 to 530 height steps per biome (0.5 m steps at 120 m Max height).

Water

Ocean, Lake, River, Pond and Swamp are water biomes. Like every biome their brightness is the height of the bed (sea floor, lake bottom, river bed). The water surface on top is worked out for you:

  • Ocean: the surface is Sea level (Map settings). Auto biome makes everything below it Ocean.
  • Lake / Pond: each connected patch fills up to its spill level, the lowest ground around its edge (at least 0.3 m deep). Bed that sticks out above it is dry shore. Paint the bed lower than the banks to get a deep lake.
  • River: 0.8 m over the bed, and always flowing downhill toward where it meets Ocean, Lake or Pond (a dip in the bed becomes a calm pool, never water running uphill).
  • Swamp: wet ground with a 0.25 m film of standing water.

The 3D view shows the water see-through, tinted per type (toggle in Map settings). Export includes the water surface per sample.

Tools

  • Raise / Lower brighten or darken the pixels and keep their hue. Smooth blurs heights. Flatten pulls toward the height where the stroke started, which is good for plateaus and build sites.
  • Biome repaints the hue with the selected swatch and keeps each pixel's brightness (height). Fill does that for a connected area of one biome. Pick takes the biome under your finger.
  • Generate: seeded noise as a rough start. Auto biome: hues from height and slope.
  • Map: width and height in pixels, meters per pixel (with presets for a 2000 m world), max height, sea level and chunk size. The top-left line shows the world size and what the meshes will cost.
  • 3D: the live low-poly preview at full resolution (one vertex per pixel, exactly what you export), flat shaded, drawn with each biome's game color. Very big maps (millions of pixels) can be slow on phones; desktops handle 2000 × 2000 fine.
  • File: save / open the map PNG, the palette, a grey heightmap. Export: a C header (or a compact .bin for big maps) with the full-resolution grid and the palette for your game. Formats: uint16 height + uint8 biome, uint8 height + uint8 biome, or packed uint16 (height in the top 12 bits, biome in the low 4: h = v >> 4, b = v & 15).

First person (Player)

To check the map at the size your player sees it: press Player, then click / tap a spot on the map or in the 3D view. The 3D view becomes the player's eyes at full resolution; a red dot with a view cone follows you on the map (and on the small map in the corner).

  • Desktop: click the 3D view to look with the mouse (or drag), WASD / arrows move, Shift sprints, Space jumps. Esc or Exit first person goes back to the orbit view.
  • Touch: left thumb is a joystick (push it all the way to sprint), drag on the right half to look, Jump button, Exit first person at the top.
  • You walk on the exact mesh and can't leave the map. Water: you wade until it is deeper than eye height − 0.3 m, then swim with your eyes 0.3 m above the surface at half speed. With Chunks on, chunk lines are drawn on the ground.
  • Map settings: eye height (1.7 m), vertical FOV (70°, with the horizontal equivalent), walk speed (5 m/s), sprint (2×), near / far clip (0.1 m / auto). Match them to your game camera.
  • Edge fog (button in the 3D view, width in Map settings, off by default) fades the last 150 m toward the map edge into light haze, in both views, with haze beyond the edge.

Controls

  • Map: one finger / left mouse paints. Two fingers pan and pinch-zoom. Mouse: right or middle drag pans, wheel zooms. Zoom in far enough and a pixel grid appears, so you can paint single pixels (brush size 1). F fits the map, = zooms to 1 screen pixel per map pixel.
  • 3D: one finger / left drag orbits, two fingers pan and zoom. Mouse: right drag pans, wheel zooms. First person: see above.
  • Keys: Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y undo / redo, [ and ] brush size, 1-9 and 0 pick a biome (in swatch order: 1 = Ocean ... 0 = Forest).

Coordinates

Z is up. The image is a top-down map with north (+Y) at the top: pixel (col, row) sits at world x = col × m/px, y = (H − 1 − row) × m/px, z = height.
